Mr. President, Mr. Secretary General, Excellencies, Ladies and Gentlemen, San Marino is the world's oldest republic. From longer than 17 centuries, we have survived not through military power, work, territorial expansion, economic might, but through dialogue, respect for the law, neutrality, and then conviction that sovereignty of a small state is no less valuable than that of a great power. Our history has taught a simple lesson. When power is not enough to protect you, rule matter. When you cannot impose your will, dialogue matter. And when the world is divided, institution that brings together matter. This is why multilateralism is not an abstract concept for San Marino. It's a fundamental principle of our foreign policy and, in many ways, a lesson drew from our own history. Our commitment to cooperation and shared rule is also reflected in our relationship with the European Union. The forthcoming signing of the Association Agreement with the European Union on 19 October will mark a milestone for our country. It represents an historical step toward closer cooperation, shared rule, and greater integration within the European Union. while fully preserving our sovereignty, identity, and institutional specificity. For San Marino, this is not simply an agreement about economic relations. It is a long-term choice to deepen our engagement with Europe while preserving the distinctive character of our republic. It is a choice that reflects a principle at the heart of our history. does not diminish sovereignty, for a small state, cooperation, rule, and dialogue are precisely what allow sovereignty to be exercised and protected. Today, that principle is under extraordinary pressure. 81 years ago, the United Nations was created to save succeeding generations from the scourge of war. Yet today, conflicts are multiplying. Inequality are deepening. Climate disruption is accelerating. Food and water are increasing, being used as an instrument of pressure, and the rules that have governed international relations for decades are being challenged. We are witnessing the use of force against the sovereign state. We are witnessing violation of international law, humanitarian, and human right law. At such a moment, the answer cannot be less multilateralism. It must be more. And it must be a multilateralism that works, that protects the weak as well as the strong. on that apply the law equality and one that give every state, large or small, a meaningful voice. Mr. President, there can be no word in which my make right. Sovereignty, territorial integrity, political independence and self-determination matter. For San Marino this is not an abstract principle. For small-state international law is our protection. It is the ultimate equalizer. A rule-based international order allows every state, large or small, to have an equal voice, an equal legal standing. This is why San Marino strongly supports the Secretary General UN8 initiative. The United Nations must become more effective, more efficient, and more responsive. and reform must include the Security Council. The Council must better reflect today's work. San Marino is also a member of the Uniting for Consensus group and supports its effort to promote a comprehensive and balanced reform of the Security Council based on a greater regional representation, equitable geographical distribution, and wider access to elected seats. Reform must train the voice of all member states, including the small states, and it must be achieved through broad consensus.
