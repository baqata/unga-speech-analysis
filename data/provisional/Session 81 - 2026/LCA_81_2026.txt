The Assembly will hear an address by His Excellency Philippe Pierre, Prime Minister, Minister for Finance, Constituency Development, People Development, Justice and National Security of St. Lucia. I request the protocol to escort His Excellency and invite him to address the Assembly. Mr. President, Mr. Secretary General, Excellencies, distinguished delegates. In 1979, 34 years after the creation of this United Nations, my country, St. Lucia, though small in geography but large in courage, dignity and conviction, took its seat among the community of nations brimming with hope, optimism and trust in the promise and goals of this body. Today, we meet at a moment of profound international uncertainty. Conflict is spreading, trust is eroding, developmental gains are being reversed, and the rules intended to protect humanity are too often applied selectively. For small island developing states, these crises are not abstract. They are lived realities. Climate change threatens our homes, livelihoods, and very existence. Death limits our ability to invest in people. Digital technologies are transforming our societies without safeguards. They can reproduce old patterns of domination in a new form. and in our own Caribbean neighborhood, people of Haiti continue to endure a humanitarian and security crisis that demands more than statements of concern. The theme before us, Restoring Trust, Managing Transformation, a United Nations that Delivers for All, must not remain a slogan. Trust will be restored only when promises are matched by action, when commitments are honoured, and when the United Nations delivers meaningful results for every nation, and when there is due regard for multilateral principles not only for the most powerful but for the weakest of us as well. This assembly should not be a place where the world expresses sympathy without accepting responsibility. We need action. We need justice. And we need a renewed international combat based not on power but on principle. We need multilateralism, not unilateralism. And in the words of the former UN Secretary General, the United Nations was not created in order to bring us to heaven, but in order to save us from hell. For St. Lucia, climate change is not a distant environmental issue. It is a direct threat to national security, economic stability, food and water security, and human survival. While the world focuses on headline-grabbing events like monster hurricanes, melting glaciers, and tidal waves, climate change is affecting St. Lucia daily in small but significant ways, such as the unusual boughs of drought in the rainy season, unprecedented floods in the dry season, and stifling of our beaches and coastlines by sargasm. Our people contribute liquor to these global emissions, yet these events constantly set back our people's development and livelihoods and demand that we find the resources to reverse the damage. St. Lucia calls for climate finance that matches the scale of the crisis, expanded grants and concessional funding, fast and simple access, full support for loss and damage, meaningful debt relief, and financing that reaches vulnerable people directly. Climate finance is not charity. It's about justice, responsibility, and survival. We must stop confusing loans through solidarity. A loan is not justice when a small, vulnerable nation is forced to borrow to repair damage it did not cause. A United Nations that delivers for all must deliver for those on the front lines of climate change, through accessible finance, concrete support and measurable results, not merely through declarations. St. Lucia amends the continued conflicts and human sufferings across the globe, including Ukraine, Sudan, and the wider Middle East, including Gaza, and calls for renewed efforts towards peace, stability, and respect for international law. In accordance with the right to self-determination and UN resolutions, in particular Resolution 326 of 1974, the people of Palestine should be allowed their own state and full membership of the UN alongside the State of Israel. We also speak today about Haiti. Member States of this organization must act in a manner that will restore the trust of the people of Haiti in the United Nations. Some Member States have failed to meet their commitments and pledges to the gang suppression force in Haiti, whose one-year mandate ends this month. The Security Council is urged to renew the force's mandates and Member States to provide the necessary resources to operate effectively and successfully. While gang violence is the main factor to be resolved in Haiti, the crisis in this country is multidimensional. The international response must include long-term social and economic assistance that will help restore an acceptable quality of life for the people of Haiti. This is why the Caribbean community has been actively engaged in Haiti. Haiti is a Caribbean country, is a Caribbean community member state. to assist with a solution to the political and governance dimensions of the crisis. Through its eminent persons group, comprising three prime ministers, it will continue to facilitate dialogue amongst Haiti's political and civil society stakeholders as they work towards holding the first round of general elections this year. Trust in the United Nations will be restored if the daily lives of the people of Haiti are transformed and enhanced. It is a similar quest for trust and transformation that St. Lucia believes there should be dialogue and the removal of all obstacles that hinder the true development of the people of Cuba. Cuba has long been celebrated for its continued contribution to health, education, and development across our region and beyond. St. Lucia stands with the Cuban people in their hope for a better future, supported by the friendship and solidarity of the international community. In the same spirit of transformation and consistent with our commitment to the right of all people to self-determination, St. Lucia supports Taiwan's full membership in the United Nations. St. Lucia believes that a truly inclusive United Nations must ensure that its decision-making bodies reflect the world that they serve. St. Lucia advocates for comprehensive reform of the Security Council to enhance its legitimacy and effectiveness in addressing global challenges, thereby restoring trust and a more inclusive multilateral system. Mr. President, a 21st century United Nations Security Council could have been more successful in its responsibility for international peace and security had it not been anchored in the world of 1945. The prolonged reform process must be concluded quickly and include expanded pullment and non-pullment membership for Africa, the Asia-Pacific, and Latin America and the Caribbean regions. This reform is essential to restore trust in the United Nations and ensure it delivers peace and security for all. St. Lusa pledges its commitment and dedication to work with like-minded nations to achieve the stated reforms. I wish to take this opportunity, Mr. President, to thank the outgoing Secretary General, Antonio Guterres, for his stirling service, especially his steadfast solidarity with the Caribbean through the many challenges and disasters our region has faced. We commend and thank him for his sincere and positive leadership of this organization. In the same vein, St. Lucia wishes to express his endorsement of the candidate of the Republic of Guyana, Ms. Caroline Rodriguez-Bouquet, for the post of Secretary General. Her distinguished record of public and international service speaks directly to the kind of leadership this moment demands. On the 25th of March, 2026, the General Assembly approved one of its most historic and consequential resolutions that declared the trafficking of enslaved Africans and racialized, shattled enslavement of Africans as the greatest crime against humanity. This resolution and the subsequent establishment of the High-Level Global Advisory Council on Reparatory Justice is a very significant step in the search for reparations for the transatlantic slave trade and the enslavement of Africans, Indian Americans, and the Caribbean. I want to thank the President of Ghana for spearheading and championing this resolution. St. Lucia congratulates the Government of Jamaica on a successful petition to the British monarch for him to refer three questions to the Judical Committee of the Privy Council over the legality of the enslavement of Africans in Jamaica. Mr. President, reparations are not about vengeance. They are about truth, acknowledgement, repair, and transformation. Reparations are not about seeking charity or revisiting history for its own sake. History is not behind us when its consequences remain embedded in wealth, power, institutions, and opportunity. Reparations are about addressing the continuing effects of slavery and colonial exploitation, restoring dignity, advancing development, and building more equitable relationships with the states and institutions that benefited from these systems. Mr. President, technology is the new form of power that is rapidly transforming the world. And while it offers enormous opportunities for mankind, it can also create a new hierarchy in which a small number of corporations and powerful states control the systems on which the rest of humanity depends. We must ensure that artificial intelligence does not widen the gap between the powerful and the vulnerable. Technology must strengthen aspirations, not quietly erode them. St. Lucia therefore calls for strong global standards on data protection, privacy and artificial intelligence, equitable access to digital infrastructure, cyber security and affordable connectivity. Fair technology transfer, transparent protection from surveillance, disinformation and cyber exploitation, and meaningful participation for small states in shaping global digital roles. The digital future must not be owned by a few. It must be governed for the benefit of all. Managing transformation means ensuring that no nation is left powerless in the face of technological change. Mr. President, my country, St. Lucia, is a small, open, and vulnerable state, but our people refuse to impose limits on their aspirations. History has blessed us with unimaginable natural beauty, ambitious friendly people, and two Nobel laureates. However, an unfair international financial system threatens the very existence of small states like St. Lucia. The rules governing global finance affect our development, and our attempt at economic diversification is hindered by unfair and unequally applied rules. Oceans of water separate us from the global powers, but our challenges are mainly connected to decisions made in the capitals. Emissions released from these countries affect our climate. Weapons and drugs trafficked from international networks instigate violence and gang warfare in our region. Lack of access to affordable technology slows our development. Mr. President, St. Lucia refuses to give up. We are educating our people to ensure that their skills are relevant. We are modernizing agriculture to ensure our food security. We are dealing with the scarcity of water due to climate change by exploring new sources of groundwater supply and encouraging harvesting of rainwater. We are reducing youth unemployment for the creation of a successful youth economy agency. We are encouraging the spirit of entrepreneurship by supporting small and micro enterprises, especially women-owned businesses. We are promoting healthy lifestyles and universal health care for the provision of equitable, accessible, and affordable health care for our citizens. Mr. President, St. Lucia continues to insist that governance must be about the improvement of the lives of the people. We have maintained fiscal discipline, reduced unemployment, as we continue to create new opportunities and an enabling environment for investment in tourism and other productive sectors. Solution believes that a better world is within our reach if we chose cooperation over coercion and ensure that justice and fairness prevail for all. The 81st session of the UN General Assembly must be remembered for turning its laudable theme into actionable commitments and those commitments into meaningful change. As Mahatma Gandhi says, in a gentle way you can shape the world. The people of St. Lucia remain confident in our collective capacity to shape that better world, even as St. Lucian Nobel laureate Sir Derek Walcott reminds us that the future happens no matter how much we scream. Our task is to meet that future with courage and purpose. Our other Nobel laureate, Sir Alfred Lewis, said, economic development requires taking risks and embracing change. And with the speed of Julian Alfred, let us embrace that responsibility together and act with the resolve this moment demands. I thank you. On behalf of the Assembly, I wish to thank the Prime Minister, Minister for Finance, Constituency Development, People Empowerment, Justice and National Security of Saint Lucia.
