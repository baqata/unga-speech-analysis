Mr. President and Excellencies, today's security landscape is not only by conflict, but by climate-driven fragility. Extreme weather events, sea level rise, and resource degradation are eroding stability across vulnerable regions, especially the Pacific. These pressures intensify humanitarian needs, disrupt food and water systems, and strain governments' capacities. For Micronesia, climate impact are a national concern. We underscore the importance of safeguarding maritime security across the Blue Pacific. Illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing activities, transnational crimes and environmental degradation threatened not only our economies but the health of our marine environment as well. We urge the international community to enhance cooperation, shared technology, and support capacity building to ensure that the ocean remains a zone of peace governed by international law. Mr. President, our people are the most important and most valuable national resource. We remain committed to delivering effective public services and advancing Micronesia priorities for our citizens, wherever they reside. Micronesia will strengthen the protection, engagement, and well-being of its citizens, including members of diaspora. Micronesia also aligns itself with the Global Compact for Safe, Orderly, and Regular Migration and welcomes its comprehensive framework for promoting humane, orderly, and well-managed international migration. Mr. President and Excellencies, our people's health has been increasingly affected by more frequent tropical storms, heat waves, and changing tidal patterns. We are seeing a rise in illnesses linked to marine life and factor-borne diseases, in particular dengue and Zika. Already, the burden of non-communicable disease strains our health system, reduces workforce productivity, and diverts limited resources. We are strengthening our national health capacity, increasing investment in diagnostic and life-saving medicine, expanding the use of telehealth to access specialist care and medical education, and partnering with neighboring countries to support regular medical exchange programs that augment our treatment capability in-country. These efforts are essential to safeguard our communities as climate impacts intensify. Mr. President, the ocean is the heart of Micronesian identity, livelihood, and future prosperity. It faces mounting pressure from climate change, sea level rise, pollution, and unsustainable exploitation. Micronesia calls for a collective effort to fortify ocean governance, sustainable fishing management and effective conservation of marine biodiversity. Being the first country to sign the BPNJ agreement, we are among the first movers comprising six nations to implement the BPNJ. Indonesia is partnering with GIZ and five co-leading states to develop sea-based management tools and marine protected area proposals under the PB&J framework, translating our commitment to conserve 30% of our ocean into concrete, durable protection for areas beyond national jurisdiction. Mr. President, on deep sea mining in the international seabed area, Micronesia employs a precautionary approach. We stress the need to consolidate comprehensive knowledge, data, and science about the marine environment before any decision is taken on proceeding with exploitation. Additionally, it is crucial that all stakeholders, including the coastal states, indigenous people, and local communities in the Pacific are meaningfully consulted prior to such a decision. Mr. President, we must address the growing threat of plastic pollution that degrades the Pacific ecosystem and communities. marine debris, microplastics, and associated toxic substances are accumulating on our shores. Our lagoons, for example, in the state of Chuk, where oil leaks from World War II remnants led to the emergency declaration last year, continue to threaten our livelihood. This impact imposed disproportionate cleanup and economic burdens on small island governments whose waste management infrastructure is inadequate. Our national efforts must be matched by strengthening international cooperation, including technical assistance, predictable finance, and collective actions across the plastic lifecycle to prevent pollution at its source and protect the Blue Pacific for present and future generations. Micronesia calls on the international community to match local action with global responsibility. Through sustained funding, meaningful technology transfer, stricter upstream control on plastic production, and an end to waste exporting. Critically, the international community must complete its work on a new treaty to end plastic pollution, including the marine environment. Mr. President and Excellencies, with less than five years to achieve the Sustainable Development Goals, the international community must respond with renewed political resolve, scaled-up financing, and accelerated implementation. The Pacific's experience underscores this imperative. Climate impacts are steadily eroding, development gains and placing progress across the goals further at risk. The recent typhoon and severe flooding in our states and more frequent king tides affecting our low-lying islands demonstrate that these are no longer isolated events but recurring pressures that test the resilience of our communities on the front line of climate crisis. For us, achieving the SDGs require accelerated action. We are mainstreaming gender across state and national planning, strengthening survivor-centered services, expanding women's economic empowerment, and decision-making and engaging in preventing gender-based violence and advancing equality.
